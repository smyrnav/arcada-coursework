# Narration script — Part E presentation (voiceover/video)

Approx. 4–5 minutes total. Slides in `presentation/presentation.pptx`.

## Slide 1 — Title (~30 s)

"Hi, this is my individual marketing research project. The question: how do Kesko,
S Group and Lidl position their private labels against national brands? Because I did
this individually, the research is digital — online catalogues, product pages and
delivery menus from Helsinki, collected on 24 November — not physical mystery shopping.
I want to be upfront about that. The analytical framework comes from the McKinsey
article on turning private labels into powerhouse brands."

## Slide 2 — The dataset (~30 s)

"The dataset is small and honest: eleven snack products across the three chains, three
matched price pairs against a Taffel benchmark, and twelve R-kioski menu records
comparing a central and a suburban kiosk. Every row has a source URL and a quality
flag. The Lidl rows are the weakest — their product pages have since gone offline."

## Slide 3 — Price comparison (~45 s)

"This is the core finding. In the online catalogues, the private-label salted crisps
are 23 to 33 percent cheaper per kilogram than the Taffel benchmark — Pirkka at Kesko,
Xtra and Coop at S Group. That is exactly the deliberate price gap the McKinsey article
says retailers should maintain. What it does not tell us is whether anyone buys them
because of it."

## Slide 4 — Finnish consumer evidence (~45 s)

"Because the article's surveys are from 2020 America, I looked for current Finnish
evidence. On the left, a 2025 PTT survey of almost 1,200 consumers: 63 percent agree
private labels are clearly cheaper, and 42 percent see no quality difference. On the
right, the Finnish Competition and Consumer Authority: private labels held roughly a
quarter of grocery sales value between 2016 and 2023 — stable, and lower than most of
Europe."

## Slide 5 — Europe and the framework (~40 s)

"For context: across 17 European markets, private labels reached almost 39 percent of
grocery value in 2025, according to PLMA and NielsenIQ. Finland is below that level.
The McKinsey maturity model on the right is the lens I used: from basic cheap
alternatives, to managed brand portfolios, to private labels that themselves create
store loyalty."

## Slide 6 — R-kioski (~40 s)

"Part B compared a central station kiosk with a suburban one. The surprising finding:
the Wolt menus are identical — same six filled breads, same prices, 3.90 to 4.60 euros.
In the delivered assortment, R-kioski standardises convenience rather than adapting by
location. Whether the physical stores differ, I cannot say — that would need the field
visit the original assignment asked for."

## Slide 7 — Conclusions (~30 s)

"Three takeaways. The chains run genuinely different private-label strategies. Price
gaps get products tried but don't create loyalty. And digital research is exact about
products and prices but silent about people — which is why the limitations are stated
rather than hidden. Thanks."
